## Meeting Minutes
- **Prototype & concept status**
  - Team demonstrated the exploratory campus game concept (working title: **“Campus Quest”**) that blends NPC interactions, treasure collection, and building interiors with a top-down navigation style.
  - Nice use of **paper prototyping** and combination of multiple team ideas.

- **Client feedback — sharpen the concept**
  - Be explicit about **WHO the game is for** (e.g., **ANU students**—especially newcomers—or **prospective students**) and **WHY** they would play (e.g., orientation, discovery, collection/decoration loop).
  - Clearly articulate **WHAT players do** (core loop): explore → encounter people/places → collect/record → earn badges/decor → progress toward goals.
  - Avoid UI keypress detail for now; focus on a **cohesive narrative** and short **pitch/proposal** that someone could quickly understand.

- **Use cases & modes**
  - Consider both **at-desk exploration** (virtual familiarity) and a **real-world nudge** (motivating campus visits later).
  - Option to include a **“wandering / non-game” mode** for users who just want to browse the campus without objectives.

- **Scope & feasibility**
  - Decide whether to **start with a small, well-defined slice of campus** or cover all of campus at low fidelity.
  - Align engine/platform choices to **team skills** (no mandate from client; **IP/cost** awareness required). High-fidelity **concept is acceptable** if a functional build is too heavy this semester.

- **Delivery process (lightweight)**
  - Emphasize **Design → Prototype → Test & refine** (quick concept testing with community) before committing to heavier build work.
  - If building, plan for **content curation** (places, sculptures, events), **UX/UI** (navigation, interactions, character options), and optional **analytics** later.

- **Outcome desired by client (near-term)**
  - By next week: a **concise proposal/pitch** that names the concept, target audience, player motivations, core loop, the first playable **scope/MVP slice**, and initial asset list (e.g., “X interiors, Y outdoor points, Z collectible types”). A simple demo is welcome if feasible.
